# TRAYN Nutrition launch and first 90 days brief

**Chosen to a standard. Tested before release.**

Our launch commitment is to select ingredients against documented quality standards, independently test manufactured finished batches before release, and make relevant batch results accessible.

Initial audience: regular whey buyers who want clear ingredient choices and product evidence. Validate this audience through customer interviews.

## Pre launch

### WEEKS 6-5 Define the product and the standards

Founder: confirm the product, ingredient specifications, qualified suppliers, price, stock and delivery coverage. Agree sampling, tests and release criteria with the lab and quality adviser. Marketer: interview 10-15 prospective buyers and compare 3-5 relevant brands.

### WEEKS 4-3 Build the audience and the evidence pages

Marketer: publish a tracked, opt-in waitlist. Prepare the product page, report page, FAQs, founder story and ingredient explanations. Shortlist suitable trainers or creators and prepare two weeks of launch content.

### WEEKS 2-1 Test the batch and the buying journey

Founder and quality adviser: test the finished batch, review results and record the release decision. Operations: test checkout, stock, dispatch and support. Marketer: announce the launch date once readiness is confirmed.

Release only when the batch meets criteria, reports are accessible, claims are supported and ordering and fulfilment are ready.

## Launch and post launch

### DAYS 0-7 Launch to a focused audience

Open to the waitlist, then the wider audience. Publish the matching batch report. Run a small sampling programme using released stock and tracked creator links. If budget allows, test one paid channel with a spend cap. Check orders and dispatch daily.

### DAYS 8-14 Listen and fix the first purchase experience

Follow up after delivery. Gather feedback on taste, mixability, packaging and value. Check where visitors abandon checkout, answer recurring questions and fix repeated product or delivery issues before increasing traffic.

### DAYS 15-30 Turn customer learning into improvements

Request honest reviews and permission to reuse customer content. Improve FAQs and product information. Compare waitlist, organic, paid and partner results. At day 30, agree what to continue, change or stop.

Day 0 is the first day sales open. Adjust the date if manufacturing or testing takes longer.

Content: 3 feed posts weekly, stories for questions, and opted-in waitlist and customer follow-ups.

## Days 31 to 90

### DAYS 31-60 Build repeat demand

- Improve the messages and channels that bring suitable customers; test one major change at a time.
- Use opted-in replenishment reminders based on actual usage. Continue productive gym and creator partnerships.
- Review repeat purchases among customers who have had time to finish their pack, customer issues and stock.

### DAYS 61-90 Decide what to scale

- Compare sales, acquisition costs, contribution, repeat buying and remaining stock.
- Increase spend only when economics, supply and customer experience support it.
- Plan the next batch including testing time. Add flavours only where demand supports them.

## Weekly scorecard

- **Qualified waitlist:** Opted-in prospects and their acquisition source.
- **Conversion:** Completed orders / website sessions, by source.
- **Acquisition cost:** Paid media spend / attributed new customers.
- **Contribution:** Net sales less product, fulfilment, fees, returns and marketing.
- **Repeat and service:** Eligible repeat buyers, complaints, dispatch and sellable stock.

Set targets after confirming stock, price, costs and budget. Proposed marketing allocation: 20% pre-launch, 30% launch, 30% days 15-90 and 20% reserve. Fund manufacturing, testing and baseline operations separately.

Ownership: founder and quality adviser own standards, testing and release; marketer owns calendar, content, acquisition and reporting; operations owns checkout, stock, dispatch and support.

Handoff: agree a weekly calendar, costs, owners and targets, with weekly reviews and decisions at days 30, 60 and 90.

The supporting practices must be documented and implemented before public claims. Reports apply to the sample and parameters tested.
